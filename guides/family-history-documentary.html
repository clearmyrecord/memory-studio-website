Memory Studio · Storytelling guide

Make a family history documentary worth passing down

The stories behind family photographs are often more valuable than the photographs alone. Record the context while you still can.

Begin with the storyteller, not the software

Invite a parent, grandparent or relative to choose a few photographs they care about. Ask open-ended questions and allow time for memories to surface. Don't assume a photo's date, place or meaning is obvious. A short recorded conversation can give future generations something a caption cannot.

Interview questions that spark stories

  • What did your home or neighborhood feel like when you were young?
  • Who influenced you most, and what did they teach you?
  • What was a turning point you didn't recognize at the time?
  • Which tradition, job or journey shaped the family?
  • What would you want a future grandchild to understand about this photograph?

Ask permission before recording and respect topics the storyteller prefers not to discuss. For archival interviews, capture the person's name, approximate date, location and any agreed sharing limits.

Organize events into a family timeline

Start with major life stages—childhood, meeting loved ones, moving homes, raising children, work and community life. Mix photos, home movies, scanned letters and recorded recollections, while keeping a note of where each item came from. If relatives disagree about a date or story, preserve the uncertainty rather than presenting a guess as fact.

Protect the family's privacy

Private footage can include children, vulnerable relatives and details someone never intended for public viewing. Discuss which clips may be shared with family and which should stay private. Store originals and project backups in separately controlled places. Any third-party distribution service will have its own terms, rights requirements and approval process.

Create a first chapter

Start small: an introduction, ten meaningful photos, one recorded interview and a closing reflection. Have family members review the draft before expanding it.
