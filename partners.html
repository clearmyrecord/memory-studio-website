Why this is different

Not another film library. A possible new source of documentary storytellers.

Traditional distribution begins when a film is finished. Memory Studio begins much earlier—with the people, places and experiences in someone's own media archive. A partner could help us bridge that gap responsibly.

Creator-first

A workflow for discovering scenes and telling life stories, rather than asking people to become professional editors first.

Privacy as a choice

Private family viewing remains separate from public licensing. No customer library is automatically sent to a distributor.

Opportunity to build together

Explore delivery standards, a qualified creator funnel, co-branded discovery or a tightly scoped pilot—without promising automatic acceptance.

Consent-first by design

Personal memories are not automatically public content.

Public storytelling can be powerful, but it must be deliberate. Our proposed partnership framework starts with the rights and expectations of the people whose lives appear on screen.

Separate consent

Creating a film, privately viewing it and submitting it publicly are different decisions. Distribution requires an affirmative, separate opt-in.

Clearance before submission

A proposed public pilot would review music, archival footage, third-party content, and relevant participant/likeness permissions before delivery.

Partner editorial control

The streaming service controls its own acquisition, classification, quality, review and acceptance decisions. We would not promise automatic placement.

Questions a partnership team may ask

Designed for a real conversation.

Do you have a finished slate available for licensing?

Not currently. Memory Studio is a Windows beta and the proposed Memory Studio Originals collection has not been produced or acquired. We are seeking guidance on what a qualified pilot would require before presenting films for acquisition.

Can a customer already watch privately on television?

The v45 Windows beta prepares compatible MP4 copies for USB playback and direct HDMI viewing, and includes an experimental local-network TV-browser flow. There is no native Roku, Fire TV or Apple TV application, no DRM, and no hosted private-family streaming service today.

Is public streaming upload integrated into the software?

No. The application does not currently submit films to a distributor or streaming platform. We are exploring a future approved intake or aggregator workflow, contingent on technical, legal and commercial agreement.

Who controls personal media and distribution rights?

The product is being designed around local-first user control. We would require separately documented permission and rights checks for any proposed public submission. Final legal terms for a distribution product would need to be agreed and reviewed before launch.

Are you seeking an exclusive deal or promising revenue?

No. We are interested in discussing a limited pilot, potentially nonexclusive, with terms determined by the participating teams. We are not claiming licensed titles, committed revenues, existing traffic guarantees or an executed distribution partnership.
